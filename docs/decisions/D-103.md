# D-103 — Vitals and lab results have separate entry and storage; scratch notes belong to their author

## Context

After D-102, three things were still open, and the owner asked for real entry and storage areas that make sense:

- Three synthetic office blood-pressure readings were stored as laboratory results. They appeared in the lab queue as "lab orders to acknowledge", inflated the Labs badge, and were missing from the vitals flowsheet. Nothing stopped this from happening again: `add_observation` accepted any category string and passed the raw request body through, including a client-supplied recorder name.
- Scratchpad notes were rows in `tasks` (`type = 'scratchpad'`) with no author or practice, and `GET /api/tasks?type=scratchpad` served every note to every signed-in user of every practice.
- Investigating these turned up two more storage defects of the same kind. First, the chart snapshot capped observations at 100 rows across all categories. Each vitals entry writes up to seven rows, so repeated vitals pushed lab history out of the Labs section and out of monitoring evidence. Second, care-completion's result-review rule filtered on category `"lab"` while storage writes `"laboratory"`, so it never fired for real results.

## Decision

1. **Vitals and lab results are different records with different entry paths.** Vitals are entered only through `record_vitals` (the vitals form), which validates each measurement and derives BMI and flags. The generic `add_observation` action accepts only `laboratory`, `imaging` and `procedure`. It refuses `vital-signs`, and refuses a laboratory entry whose LOINC code or name is a vital sign (`app/domain/observation-categories.ts`). The API validates the payload strictly: required fields, a collected date that is not in the future, a fixed interpretation vocabulary, and no client-supplied recorder or source.
2. **A real lab-result entry form.** The Labs section has *Record result* (for results from a faxed or portal report) and *Record vitals*. A saved result is a laboratory observation that enters the lab queue for acknowledgement. A vital sign typed into the lab form is redirected to the vitals form before it can be saved.
3. **Seeded readings are vitals.** `patientVitalHistory` seeds them in exactly the rows `record_vitals` writes. Migration `2026-09-25-001` converts the old laboratory rows of existing databases in place (same id and timestamp) with a `reclassify` provenance event.
4. **The snapshot's `observations` holds results only.** Vitals arrive summarized as `vitals` (`nonVitalObservations`). Monitoring evidence ignores vital-sign rows passed as observations, and category checks use `isLaboratoryCategory`.
5. **Scratch notes have their own table** (`scratch_notes`, migration `2026-09-25-002`), owned by an author inside a practice. They are read and deleted only by that author. A patient-tagged note shows only while its author can still open the chart, and another clinician's note answers like a missing one. Existing notes are attributed from their `scratchpad_created` audit event. The synthetic seed notes belong to the prototype provider, and the two clinical memos are tagged to Maya Chen. A note nobody can be credited with stays visible to its practice, marked "Unattributed". Notes carry a creation timestamp and the client formats it.

## Consequences

- The Labs badge and lab queue no longer count office vitals. Existing dev databases are corrected on next start.
- Care-completion now raises "Review result" items for unacknowledged laboratory results, as its rule always intended.
- The omnibox's local answers and the medication truth panel still read fixtures directly (now through `fixtureMonitoringEvidence`, which includes the vitals fixture). Moving them onto authoritative records is separate work.
