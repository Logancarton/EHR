### Decision

HR is a capability every member of the practice has, presented in two places, with a
hard authorization boundary between a person's own record and anyone else's.

**Presentation.** HR is both a contextual companion and a major workspace:

- a companion in the right rail, with the canonical
  `minimized -> docked -> expanded canvas -> redocked -> minimized` lifecycle, for
  glancing at your own deadlines without leaving what you were doing; and
- a major workspace, opened from the `+` launcher as a persistent labeled tab, for
  real HR work.

Home's three major entities stay Clinical, Billing and Brand. The `+` launcher already
offers more than those three — Calendar, Patients, Intake and Documents are all there —
so HR joining it needs no change to what Home means.

Both read the same records through the same permission-aware service. Two
presentations of one capability, not two HR implementations.

**Authorization.** Two distinct scopes:

- *Own record* — every authenticated member, always. Insurance, licensing timeline and
  deadlines, coachings, goals, and whatever else has been designated for them.
- *Other people's records* — the staff directory, credentialing, and anyone else's
  personal HR material. Restricted to organization **owners**, organization
  **managers**, and members holding an explicit **HR designation**.

The HR designation is a grantable capability rather than a clinical role. Owners and
managers hold HR access inherently; they may additionally designate a specific member
as HR personnel, so an HR administrator who is not a practice manager can be given
access to employee records without also being handed organization administration.
Clinical role never grants HR access: a physician is not an HR administrator by virtue
of being a physician, and a front-desk member with the designation is one.

**Assignment.** What an individual employee has — which licenses are tracked, which
deadlines apply, which coachings and goals are theirs — is assigned by the office
manager or the owner. An employee reads their own designated material; they do not
author their own HR record.

Implementing that settled the write side as two tiers, because they are two different
authorities:

- *Assigning* — setting up a record and adding what it holds. The same grant as reading
  other people's records: owners, managers, and a designated HR administrator. Doing HR
  administration is what the designation is for, and an administrator who could read
  every record but not record a renewal date would be a half-role. A member without the
  grant cannot author HR material at all, including their own — which is the rule the
  paragraph above exists to protect.
- *Designating* — granting or revoking HR access itself. Owners and managers only. A
  designated HR administrator deliberately cannot designate anyone else: an access
  grant that can reproduce itself without the practice's administrators is not a
  boundary. Owners and managers hold HR access inherently, so the designation is
  refused for them rather than offered as a toggle that appears to grant what is
  already held and to revoke what it cannot take away.

### What this amends in D-085

D-085 states that Staff/People/HR is "deliberately not a Home entity and is not a major
`+` workspace" and "belongs in the contextual companion/canvas layer."

That placement is amended. HR is now both a companion **and** a major workspace. The
rest of D-085 stands unchanged: Home's major entities remain Clinical, Billing, and
Brand, the additive-first migration rule still governs every removal, and the companion
lifecycle contract is unchanged — HR's companion presentation is held to it.

The original reasoning was that HR is contextual rather than a place clinicians live.
That reasoning held while HR meant *the staff directory*, which is administrative and
belongs to a few people. It does not hold once HR also means *your own licensing
deadlines, insurance, coachings and goals*, which is everybody's and is worth a
durable tab. The amendment follows the change in what HR is, not a reversal of the
shell's direction.

### Why the boundary is architectural rather than presentational

The surface this replaces, `HRStaffWorkspace`, rendered a hard-coded roster of every
employee's NPI, DEA number, license expirations, malpractice policy and FTE to any
authenticated user, with no permission check of any kind. Nothing real leaked, because
the roster was fixture literals — but a screen whose only protection is that its data
is fake is not a screen that can later be given real data.

Employee HR material is personnel data: immigration status, insurance, disciplinary and
coaching history, compensation-adjacent facts. It carries its own legal obligations,
separate from PHI and not covered by the clinical permission set. So the boundary is
enforced server-side in a permission-aware service, the way patient access already is,
and the UI reflects a decision the server already made rather than making it.

Consistent with the project's existing rule: never substitute fixture literals for
authorized source data, and never let a successful-looking fallback stand in for a
retrieval the caller was not permitted to make.

## Consequences

- A new grantable capability expresses HR access. It is granted by organization owners
  and managers, held inherently by them, and never implied by clinical role.
- HR records become owned domain data with a permission-aware service boundary, rather
  than component-local fixtures.
- HR joins the shared workspace catalog, so Home, the `+` launcher and the tab strip
  agree about it without a fourth hard-coded destination list.
- The existing `hr` module's fixture roster is replaced rather than extended. Until the
  authorized service exists behind it, an unauthorized viewer sees an explicit refusal,
  not a reduced-looking version of someone else's record.
- Real personnel data remains prohibited until the production-readiness gate in
  `AGENTS.md` is satisfied, exactly as PHI is. Synthetic fixtures stay clearly separated
  from the authorized access path.
- Once records can be assigned, the development seed can no longer rewrite them. HR
  items carry their origin (`hr_record_items.source`), the seed refreshes only the rows
  it authored, and it applies a designation only when first establishing a record. A
  fixture that re-asserted itself on every boot would delete an assignment and revoke a
  real authority grant — the same failure as substituting fixture literals for
  authorized data, arriving from the other direction.
