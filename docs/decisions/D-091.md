# D-091 — A standing failure record names a cause per failure; the demo practice is placed by the relationships it was written to express; and the launcher lists what is open

Decision ID: D-091
Date: 2026-09-21
Architectural status: Active / Accepted
Implementation status: Implemented in CB-0b. Three stale specs were repaired against the surfaces that replaced the ones they named, the demo schedule's placement was changed at the seeding boundary, the `+` launcher now offers open charts first, and the browser spec that books into the shared practice takes its bookings back out. ## Decision record

Date: 2026-09-21.

Status: accepted.

The browser gate had carried a group of failures recorded as one open defect — "the
demo fixture week moves under its specs" — across four slices. Reproduced on a deleted
database at `fd26cc3`, the group was **172 passed / 6 failed of 178**, and the six had
four different causes. Only one of them was the recorded defect.

### Decision 1 — a failure joins a recorded defect on its own evidence, never on its company

A defect entry that names a *family* rather than a *cause per member* stops being a
diagnosis and becomes a place to put red. Three of the six had nothing to do with the
fixture week and had been failing since the slice that caused each one:

- `dismissal` and `home-assistant` waited 45 seconds for `getByRole("button", { name:
  "EHR" })`. The Home tile that leads back into the clinical workspace was labelled
  "EHR" until UI-2 made Home present the three major suite entities, where it became
  **Clinical** (`8a573ee`).
- `calendar-interval-jump` drove `.interval-chip`, `.companion-active-interval-banner`
  and `.calc-days-input` — a companion-only scheduling UI that `94cdc8e` deliberately
  removed when the Calendar companion became the same `CalendarWorkspace` the Calendar
  tab renders, so that there would not be two scheduling surfaces able to disagree.

None of the three was a product defect. All three were specs left behind by a change
that was itself correct, and the family they were filed under hid that for four slices.
The roadmap's open-defect entry now carries a line per failure with the commit that
caused it, and a failure is added to it only with that line.

The rule this gives the next slice is the inverse of the one already written down. "Do
not let a recorded defect absorb a failure of your own" is not enough: a recorded defect
must also not absorb a failure of *anyone's*, including the ones already inside it.

### Decision 2 — the demo practice is placed by relationship, and a populated today outranks a weekday

The one real member of the family is now reproduced in isolation and repaired.
`tool-navigation`'s CB-3 case failed on a Playwright strict-mode violation: Jordan Reed
resolved to two `.gcal-event-row` elements in one displayed week — `apt-5`, waiting, and
`apt-mon-1`, scheduled — on a deleted database with no other spec having run.

The cause is that the fixture set expresses **two different relationships** and was
being placed with one offset. The anchor's own week is written in *days* — yesterday's
completed visits, today's clinic, tomorrow — and the block after it is written in
*weeks*: "Upcoming Week", a second visit for a patient who was already seen, deliberately
on the far side of a week boundary. A single day shift keeps the first relationship and
destroys the second on every day of the year except a Friday.

Each block is therefore placed by the relationship it was written to express:

- The anchor's week keeps the day shift, so **today is always the anchor day**. A first
  install opens on a populated clinic with yesterday behind it.
- A later week is placed that many weeks after *today's* week, keeping its authored
  weekday. Next week's Monday is a Monday next week, on any day of the year.

The roadmap posed this as a choice between shifting by whole weeks and making the specs
stop naming weekdays. Shifting by whole weeks was rejected on inspection: it restores
weekday identity but leaves "today" empty on the two weekdays the fixture set does not
use, and an empty clinic day is the one thing a demo practice must never hand someone who
has just installed it. What it protects — the weekday a fixture was written for — exists
only in comments and row ids. Nothing a clinician sees names it.

So **weekday identity is deliberately not preserved**: an install on a Sunday gets a
Sunday clinic. That is the cost, it is stated rather than discovered, and the specs stop
naming weekdays as its consequence — `tool-navigation` now reaches the active clinic day
through `.gcal-week-header-col.is-today`, which is what its own comment always said it
wanted.

Verified over 400 consecutive clinic days: today is the six-visit clinic on every one of
them, no two fixtures collide on a slot, and the patient with a visit today and one next
week is never drawn into a single displayed week.

### Decision 3 — a browser spec that writes into the shared practice owns what it wrote

The suite shares one database across every spec in a run. `care-completion` books real
follow-ups four weeks after an origin visit it dates a few weeks back, for two patients
who are already on the demo schedule — so its bookings land inside the demo practice's
current week and stay there. In the full run that produced a *third* Jordan Reed at
11:00 AM, which is the difference between `tool-navigation` failing in isolation and
failing for two unrelated reasons at once.

Sharing the database is not itself the defect to fix here, and isolating it is a larger
change than this slice. The rule that costs nothing is that a spec which writes future
visits into the shared practice removes them again. `care-completion` now does. Its
origin visits are left in place deliberately: they are dated weeks in the past, they
carry the encounter the board reads, and they sit outside every week another spec looks
at.

### Decision 4 — the `+` launcher's patient list is ordered by what is open, not by name

The last of the six looked like flake and was not. `workspace-open-launcher` failed only
in full runs and passed alone, which had been filed under the shared database for three
slices. The cause is exact: `OpenWorkspaceLauncher` offered `roster.slice(0, 5)`, and
`PatientRepository` orders the roster by name. On the demo roster Maya Chen is **fifth of
six**, so any patient a run registers whose name sorts before hers pushed the chart under
test off the end of the list.

That is a product defect rather than a test artefact, and it breaks UI-1's own rule.
"If already open, focus the existing tab instead of duplicating it" cannot hold for a
chart that is not on the list, and whether a chart was on the list depended on the
patient's initial. With a real roster, most of a clinician's open charts would simply not
be offered.

The list is therefore ordered by the clinician's work rather than by the alphabet: the
active chart first, then the other open charts, then the roster fills the remaining
places, still capped at five. Sorting is stable, so the roster's own order survives
inside each group. `an open chart is offered even when its patient sorts past the end of
the list` guards it with Sofia Martinez, who sorts last on the demo roster; it fails
against the old ordering.

The section keeps its "Recent Patients" heading. Ordering by what is open is the closest
honest reading of recency the launcher has today, and renaming it would be a separate
call with no functional content.

### Consequences

- The recorded fixture-week defect is closed, and the full browser suite is green at
  179/179 for the first time since CB-5a. What remains of the original six is
  `synthetic-visit`, an intermittent that fails only under full-suite load and has
  passed in the two runs since; it is not declared repaired, and the shared mutable
  database it points at is the next bounded repair.
- The demo practice now behaves the same on every day of the year, which is what makes a
  browser run's result mean the same thing on every day of the year.
- `seededScheduleDate` is the one place that decides placement, and it now states both
  relationships and the trade it makes between them.
