# D-116 — Patient record tools in the persistent right companion

Decision ID: D-116
Date: 2026-10-03
Status: Accepted
Amends: [D-096](D-096.md), [D-098](D-098.md)

## Owner direction

The primary chart's left navigation duplicates the right companion strip. Medications, Labs, Documents, Messages, History and Orders belong in that always-discoverable strip. Overview and Encounter remain visible in the patient header. Patient information, worklist pinning and column comparison retain labeled header access; detached charts and saved legacy section routes remain supported.

## Interaction and ownership

These six clinical tools remain present even when an older saved pin layout omits them. Optional companions retain clinician pin choices. The strip retains accessible names and adds visible short labels for record tools. A hidden strip retains its existing visible recovery handle.

Opening a record tool on a foreground chart selects that patient. A subsequent foreground chart switches the view to that patient. Moving to Dashboard, Home, Calendar or another practice canvas retains the open tool's explicit patient identity, including name, MRN and date of birth. Closing the tool ends that selection; reopening on a practice canvas asks for a patient. Expand/redock preserves the same instance and target. An unavailable patient disappears from the picker and cannot become a fallback to another chart.

`useCompanionPatientSelection` owns only open-tool selection. It derives the next chart synchronously, then retains that selection through practice navigation. It creates no patient record, authorization cache or navigation owner. Prescribing also uses this follow rule; its practice queue remains available through its explicit selector.

Medication, document, message and history panels reuse the existing clinical surfaces. Labs retains its existing results, monitoring, ordering and practice queue, and adds the existing chart's result-entry/review surface. Orders opens the existing patient-bound staging/review composer. No transport or clinical write is inferred from opening or expanding a tool.

Messages without a selected chart retains the existing intake-contact recipient picker and its administrative permission boundary. Selecting a charted patient binds the shared frame; closing ends the tool selection.

## Clinical boundary

Views follow the chart; consequential drafts do not change their patient. Message replies remain keyed by patient and thread in the existing working-data stores. Lab forms are keyed by patient within the open Labs tool. Staged orders and open order composers retain their originating patient through the existing order owner. Switching views does not commit a record.

Explicit retained patient actions may use their existing server-validated patient binding from a practice canvas. Labs opts into this limited exception to D-098's foreground gate; its unbound state and a manually selected target different from a foreground chart remain gated. Clinical AI and assessment insertion retain their existing parked/foreground requirements. History insertion opens the selected patient's Encounter and requires another explicit Insert action before dispatching the existing patient-bound insertion event.

## Limits and validation

Labs monitoring uses the retrieved active medication records, rejects foreign-patient payloads and cancelled responses, and exposes loading/failure instead of a successful-looking monitoring status. Published workspace chrome geometry converts enlarged DOM bounds back to root layout coordinates so companion tools begin immediately below the tabs at 200% CSS enlargement.

This changes presentation, tool selection and action routing, not clinical data ownership, semantic AI or vendor transport. Original chart routes remain for saved sections, detached panes and deep links. Closing a lab tool ends its local form lifecycle; durable staged orders survive. The repository's shared browser database remains a test-isolation risk. Completion evidence and any remaining limitations belong in ROADMAP_COMPLETED and ROADMAP.
