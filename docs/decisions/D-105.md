## Context

The owner asked for a billing workflow tab like Intake's. Billing had two flat tables: one of charges and one of signed encounters with no charge. Neither said which piece of work was stuck, why, or whose move it was. The same gap had been closed in Intake with a staged queue: stage tabs with counts, sort and search, cards showing progress and the next blocker, and a detail panel with a step timeline.

## Decision

1. **Billing gains a Workflow view** beside Charges and Practice setup. Charges stays the default until the workflow proves parity, following the rule that a replacement coexists with the route it may later replace.
2. **The queue is a pure projection** (`app/domain/billing-workflow.ts`) over the one `/api/billing` load the Charges view already makes. It adds no table, no endpoint and no second billing state. The encounter is the unit of work, so a row keeps its identity and its selection when a charge is prepared from it.
3. **Stage follows the server's review rule.** "Ready for review" is exactly `billingChargeIsReviewable`, the check the review action enforces, so the queue cannot call a charge ready when the server would refuse it.
4. **Unknown is not missing.** Before a charge is prepared, its diagnoses, coverage and fees have not been read from the signed record. Those steps are `pending`, not `needed`.
5. **Non-blocking attention.** Absent coverage and unpriced lines are raised as "check" items. They do not block review, matching the server. They are never rendered as self-pay or $0.00.
6. **No transmission step can complete.** Submission is always `not_available`, carries the transport's reason, and is excluded from progress. This holds even if a transport reports ready, because submission itself is still refused.
7. **One owner per action.** Prepare, review and void are single handlers in `BillingWorkspace`, used by both views. The queue reuses Intake's CSS vocabulary rather than creating a parallel one.

## Consequences

- A biller can see at a glance what is waiting, what is stuck on the clinician, and what is done. Each step names where its fix lives.
- Removing either Billing table in favour of the queue is a later, separate step. Parity is not yet claimed.
- The queue reuses Intake's class names, so a change to Intake's queue styling also changes Billing's. That is intended while the two are the same interaction. If they diverge, extract a shared queue primitive then.
