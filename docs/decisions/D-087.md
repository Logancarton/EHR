## Decision record

Date: 2026-09-21.

Status: accepted.

### Decision

The surface behind `Practice -> Practice settings` is **organization administration**, and
it lives in the **account menu**, offered only to an actor holding `manage_organization`.

It is not a preferences screen, and it is not part of HR.

### What this amends

D-085 lists Practice's decomposition as "Billing to its major workspace, Website/Social
Media to Brand, Staff/HR to companion/canvas, **Settings to profile/preferences**, and
Reports to an owning workspace when real." That line described the child by the name the
menu gave it. The name was wrong, and planning from it produced a slice that did not exist:
a three-way separation of preferences, organization administration and HR.

Verified in the implementation at `6981f52`:

- The `settings` module renders `PracticeStaffWorkspace` — provisioning a user, clinical
  role, membership role, membership status, patient-access scope, activation-link issuance,
  login-lockout clearing, and deactivate/reactivate. That is administration of the
  organization's own people and their access.
- It does **not** own the practice's default layouts. Those already live under
  profile/preferences: `WorkspaceProfileMenu`, `WorkspaceTopBar` and
  `PresetManagementModal` consume `app/lib/workspace-templates.ts` against
  `/api/organization/workspace-templates`. The "preferences" half of the old framing rested
  on a role-*hint string* inside the screen describing what an owner can do elsewhere.
- There is no HR half. HR (D-086) owns personnel material — insurance, licensing deadlines,
  coachings, goals — behind `manage_hr`. This owns accounts and access behind
  `manage_organization`. The two describe the same people and share no data, no service and
  no permission.

So D-085's "Settings to profile/preferences" is amended to: **organization administration
moves to the account menu**. Layout configuration was already under profile/preferences and
did not move at all, which is what D-083 recorded and remains true.

### Why the account menu rather than the Preferences menu beside it

Both live in the top-right utility cluster, and either would have satisfied a literal
reading of D-085.

The Preferences menu is the workspace's layout control: customize, reset, practice layouts,
saved layouts. Putting the administration of accounts and access inside it would rebuild
the exact conflation this decision exists to dissolve — a menu that is mostly "how my
screen looks" with one item that provisions colleagues and mints credentials.

The account menu already states the server-verified identity and role that every clinical
action uses. Administration of who else holds an identity in this practice belongs beside
that statement. It is also not a Home entity and not a major `+` workspace, so the shell
invariants are unchanged: Home remains Clinical, Billing and Brand.

### Authority

The entry is rendered only when the session's permission list — the server's own answer,
from `permissionsForActor` via `/api/auth/me` — contains `manage_organization`, which is
held inherently by organization owners and managers and is never implied by clinical role.

Hiding the entry is **not** the boundary. `/api/organization/members` refuses without the
permission, with a `403` and an explicit message, on both reading the roster and
provisioning a person. The hidden control is the product declining to advertise a door that
will not open; the refusal is what makes it a door.

This matters more here than for most surfaces because provisioning issues an **activation
link** — a response that carries a secret, shown once because the server stores only a
hash. The migration rule was that the new home must not be reachable more loosely than the
retired one. It is reachable more strictly: the old menu entry was rendered for everyone
and relied entirely on the server's refusal.

### The rename

The destination is called **Organization administration** in the tab, the workspace header
and the account menu, and **Organization** where a short label is needed. The tool registry
called it "Settings" and hinted "Preferences and account", which was part of why the menu
looked like it held a preferences screen.

The module id stays `settings`. Saved rails and the persisted module view name it, and
renaming what a thing is called is not a reason to invalidate a clinician's workspace. The
id is an internal address; the label is the product's claim about what the screen does, and
only the claim was wrong.

### Consequences

- UI-6 closes. Every Practice child has a verified owner, and the Practice group is gone
  from the top work navigation.
- Reports left with the group rather than needing a home: the tool registry calls it
  `planned` and the menu filtered on that, so it never rendered a destination.
- The tool-pin menu can still pin this destination to the left rail for any user, which is
  unchanged pre-existing behaviour and is not a leak — the server refuses. Making the tool
  registry permission-aware is a separate decision about the registry, not about this
  surface, and is deliberately not taken here.
