## Context

The Clinical AI companion answered one question at a time and replaced each answer with the next. A clinician working through a chart asks in sequences ("what is she on?", "and her last lithium level?", "draft the lab"), and the visit itself is a conversation with the patient that the scribe hears. The owner wants the assistant to work inside that flow without a second AI system and without letting speech in the room become an order.

## Decision

There is one continuous conversation per patient. Every sentence in it goes either to the patient or to the assistant, and four rules hold:

1. **Every sentence has one addressee, and the clinician sets it.** Speech to the patient is transcribed into the note and is never treated as a command. Speech to the assistant (typed, push-to-talk, or beginning "Bond, …") is acted on and kept out of the note. A model never guesses the addressee; a wrong guess could turn a patient's sentence into an order.
2. **Four kinds of output, two automatic.**
   - *Show* ("pull up her last lithium level"): happens automatically, with its source attached.
   - *Go* (opening Labs or the last note): happens automatically.
   - *Prepare* (a refill, a lab order, a message): staged as a proposal.
   - *Real* (sending, placing, signing): only a clinician's click does this, through `ClinicalActionGateway`.
3. **Approval is a click, never a spoken word.** The microphone cannot tell who said "yes": the clinician or the patient across the desk.
4. **One tray for every proposal.** It holds what the clinician asked for ("Bond, order a lithium level") and what the scribe heard ("let's go up to 100 of sertraline"). A heard proposal shows the sentence it came from and is never pre-approved.

Two supporting rules:

- **Follow-ups stay with the thread's patient.** "Her" or "that level" can only refer to the current thread's patient. Naming someone else goes through the full name and date-of-birth check and shows as a visible switch; it never silently moves the thread.
- **In the room, another chart needs typing or a click.** Pulling up a different patient's chart while a visit is live requires typed or clicked intent, because the patient may be looking at the screen.

This extends what exists; it does not add a second AI system. The existing planner gains conversation memory, the scribe's suggestions join the planner's proposals, and LIVE capture (D-115) receives spoken note instructions. Safety stays in code (patient binding, gateway, validation), not in model instructions.

## The conversation thread is not the record

The thread is a working view held in the browser session. It is never written to the chart, never sent anywhere as a transcript, and is dropped at sign-out. Anything that should outlive it goes through its owning workflow: Insert into note, a staged order, a task. This follows AI_SYSTEM.md ("Do not treat model conversation history as the patient record").

## Constraints recorded

- **Speech recognition.** Chrome's built-in speech recognition sends audio to Google by default. That is acceptable for synthetic patients only. Production needs a speech engine covered by a BAA, or one that runs locally.
- **Spending (D-107).** CONV-1 to CONV-5 need no paid services. CONV-6 (hosted semantic scribing / paid models) waits for funding.

## CONV-1 as implemented (2026-10-09)

- The Clinical AI companion keeps a thread per patient: each question and its answer stay in order. Earlier turns collapse to one line and reopen on click; the latest stays open.
- The thread's patient is sent to the planner as the active patient, so an unqualified follow-up resolves to that patient (the planner already falls back to the active patient when nobody is named).
- An answer that resolved to another named patient is labelled "This answer is about X, not Y. The conversation stays with Y." It cannot be inserted into the thread patient's note, and the thread does not move.
- A late answer lands only in the thread it was asked in, and cannot revive a cleared thread or a dismissed turn. One question at a time per thread.
- The thread survives chart switches (the panel parks, D-117), closing and reopening the panel, and expand/redock. Clear conversation empties it. Sign-out and account switch drop every thread.
- At CONV-1 the planner did not read earlier turns; CONV-1b below adds that.

## CONV-1b as implemented (2026-10-09)

- **Conversation memory.** The panel sends up to six earlier questions: the clinician's own words from turns answered about the thread's patient. It never sends answers, workspace commands, failures, clarifications, or questions answered about another patient. The server treats them only as a way to read the new request. Two follow-ups use them: "what about <patient>?" (and similar wording) repeats the last question for the patient named, and "the one before that" asks for the next-older result of the last lab question, walking further back each time it is repeated. Everything else is planned as typed. Every answer is re-read from records, the plan card shows "Read as: …", and running out of results says so and adds no evidence.
- **Named-patient check.** With a chart active, a partial name (a first name or a surname alone) that points to a different patient does not switch. The plan asks for the full name, quotes the word that was typed, and shows the match with its date of birth; nothing is answered or proposed. A full name, MRN or id switches visibly as before, and proposals for that patient stay blocked against the active chart. A date of birth in the request that belongs to exactly one match identifies that patient, in either stored form (`YYYY-MM-DD` or `MM/DD/YYYY`). Patient candidates now carry their date of birth wherever the plan lists them. The rule applies to the omnibox as well as the companion, because both use the same planner.
