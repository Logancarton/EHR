# D-117 — Clinical Bond shell ownership and unified communication

Decision ID: D-117
Date: 2026-10-03
Status: Accepted
Builds on: [D-116](D-116.md)
Amends: shell/rail placement rules in earlier workspace decisions where they conflict with this decision.

## Owner direction

Clinical Bond must have a stable ownership model for its top chrome, workspace tabs, right companion rail, and any left-side navigation. The same capability must not drift between surfaces merely because more than one surface can technically render it.

The durable rule is:

> **Top opens work. Right brings tools to the work. Left organizes one complex piece of work.**

The left side is not a permanent Clinical Bond application rail and is not a second configurable tool rail.

## Shell ownership contract

### 1. Global top bar — application-level controls only

The upper global bar owns controls that apply across the application rather than to one record or workflow:

- Clinical Bond / Home entry
- universal omnibox for search, navigation, questions and commands
- voice entry
- account / identity
- preferences and application configuration

The top bar may invoke or route to clinical actions through the omnibox, but invoking an action does not transfer ownership of that action to the top bar.

Patient Labs, Medications, Documents, Communication, Orders, Assessments, Tasks and similar contextual tools do not become permanent top-bar destinations.

### 2. Workspace tab strip and `+` — full workspaces

The browser-style tab strip represents **open work**. The `+` launcher opens or focuses full workspaces that can reasonably replace the primary canvas and remain open independently.

Examples include:

- Provider / Clinical Home or Dashboard
- Calendar
- patient charts
- Intake
- Billing
- Brand
- HR
- other genuinely practice-wide workspaces as they become authoritative

A workspace is not created merely because a feature can be expanded to fill the canvas. Expansion of a companion is a presentation change, not automatic promotion to a shell-level workspace.

### 3. Workspace-local header — a few primary modes

When one workspace has only a small number of primary modes, those modes belong in that workspace's own header rather than in a left rail.

The primary patient chart uses:

- Overview
- Encounter

These remain visible in the patient identity/header area. They are modes of the patient workspace, not companion tools and not global workspaces.

### 4. Right companion rail — contextual tools, records and supporting work

The persistent right rail owns things the clinician needs **beside the current work**.

For patient care, the canonical right-side family includes:

- Medications
- Labs
- Documents
- Communication
- History
- Orders
- Clinical AI
- Assessments / calculators

Optional contextual utilities such as Tasks and Scratchpad may also live there when they are useful beside the active work.

The companion lifecycle remains:

`minimized/icon -> docked right panel -> expanded canvas -> redocked -> minimized`

Expansion does not create a second owner or second instance. Tool identity, patient/recipient binding, drafts, selected thread/item, filters and return path must survive presentation changes.

### 5. Left side — optional local navigation for one complex workspace only

There is no permanent global left application rail.

A left-side navigation surface may exist **inside one specific workspace** only when that workspace has enough internal hierarchy to justify persistent local navigation.

Examples that may justify a local left structure:

- Billing: claims, rejected items, payments, payer work, reports
- Intake: new, incomplete, ready for review, ready to schedule, scheduled, archived
- Brand, if it grows into distinct website/social/reputation/campaign/analytics areas

The rule is:

> **A left navigation may contain children of one workspace. It may never contain peer workspaces or globally pinned tools.**

When the clinician leaves that workspace, its local left navigation disappears.

A patient chart does not use a permanent left section rail. Overview and Encounter remain in the patient header; supporting records and tools remain on the right.

## One-owner rule

Every capability has one canonical owner.

Other surfaces may show:

- summaries
- badges
- projections
- alerts
- shortcuts
- actions that open the owner

They must not become independent competing implementations of the same capability.

Examples:

- Overview may show active medications, but Medications owns the medication record and medication workflow.
- Encounter may show laboratory monitoring status, but Labs owns patient lab review/order work.
- Provider Home may show a pending communication count, but Communication owns the conversation.
- AI may summarize a prior message or result, but it does not become the record owner.

This follows the existing principle that one source owns a concern even when several surfaces project it.

## Scope rule: practice work versus patient work

The same domain may appear at different scopes only when the jobs are genuinely different. The UI must name them so clinicians are not asked to distinguish two identically named destinations.

Examples:

- **Results Queue** = practice-wide results awaiting acknowledgement.
- **Labs** = one patient's laboratory history, monitoring and ordering.
- **Document Inbox** = practice-wide inbound records/faxes/forms needing work.
- **Documents** = one patient's chart documents.
- **Prescribing Queue** = practice-wide prescriptions/refills needing attention.
- **Medications / Prescribe** = one patient's medication record and prescribing work.

A practice queue and a patient tool may share authoritative data and workflow owners, but their shell labels and context must make the scope obvious.

## Communication is one canonical capability

`Messages` and `Communication` are not separate shell capabilities.

Clinical Bond has one canonical **Communication** tool. It owns conversational/threaded communication while preserving explicit scope and transport underneath.

Communication includes, as implemented and authorized:

- patient portal/SMS/patient messaging
- staff-to-staff/team messaging
- email
- fax-related correspondence/workflow where conversational handling is appropriate
- referral and outside-clinician/community communication

The internal UI may organize Communication by scope, for example:

- Patient
- Team
- External

and/or by useful inbox/channel views where needed.

### Communication context rules

Patient and staff communication must never be silently mixed.

- Opening Communication from a foreground patient chart may default to that patient's communication context.
- Switching to Team explicitly leaves patient-thread scope.
- Team messaging must not silently inherit the foreground patient as a recipient or clinical context.
- A patient thread retains explicit patient identity.
- An internal/team thread retains explicit staff/team identity.
- An external/referral thread retains explicit outside recipient/context.
- Switching charts must not retarget a draft or send target.
- Drafts, recipients, thread identity and transport remain bound to the owner that created them until deliberately changed.

Practice-wide unread/pending communication may be projected onto Provider Home or badges, but selecting it opens the same Communication owner rather than a separate Messages implementation.

## Architectural migration direction

The current `workspace-tools` model still contains legacy concepts from the period when left and right were both configurable rails. That model must be simplified without breaking saved-state recovery or clinical workflows.

Target direction:

1. The right rail remains the configurable/persistent companion launcher.
2. Global `left[]` tool pinning is retired as a user-facing shell concept.
3. Any workspace that needs left navigation owns its own local navigation configuration and renderer.
4. `messages` and `communication` converge on one canonical Communication identity.
5. Existing routes, saved preferences and deep links are migrated safely rather than deleted abruptly.
6. Legacy IDs may be accepted temporarily as aliases during migration, but only one canonical UI owner should render.
7. Practice-wide queue names are clarified where they currently collide with patient-tool names.

## Non-goals

This decision does not require:

- redesigning the entire shell
- removing browser-style workspace tabs
- removing patient Overview or Encounter
- rewriting clinical data stores
- combining unrelated transport backends into one persistence table
- making every right companion a full workspace
- forcing a left navigation into every workspace

The goal is ownership clarity, not visual novelty.

## Decision test for future features

Use this order:

1. **Does it replace the primary work and deserve to remain independently open?** -> workspace tab / `+`
2. **Is it one of only a few primary modes of the current workspace?** -> workspace-local header
3. **Is it needed beside the current work or does it support that work?** -> right companion
4. **Does this one workspace have many internal peer areas that require persistent structure?** -> optional local left navigation
5. **Does another surface already own it?** -> show a projection/shortcut and open the existing owner; do not create a duplicate

## Acceptance criteria

The shell ownership cleanup is complete when:

- the global top bar contains only application-level controls and universal command/search entry
- the `+` launcher contains full workspaces, not arbitrary contextual tools
- the primary patient chart has no permanent left section rail
- Overview and Encounter remain patient-header modes
- patient record/support tools are consistently reachable from the right companion system
- no user-facing global left tool-pin rail remains
- local left navigation, if present, is owned by exactly one workspace and disappears outside it
- Messages and Communication no longer appear as competing shell destinations
- one Communication owner supports explicit Patient, Team and External scope without silently changing recipients/context
- practice queues and patient tools with related domains use names that clearly distinguish scope
- existing saved routes/preferences/deep links are migrated or aliased safely
- automated tests cover shell ownership, migration compatibility and patient/recipient binding safety
