# D-093 — The Prescribing companion picks a patient, and the pane it opens carries that patient's identity

Decision ID: D-093
Date: 2026-09-21
Architectural status: Active / Accepted
Implementation status: Implemented. The Prescribing companion offers a patient selector; choosing one renders the same `PatientPrescriptionWork` the chart's Medications section renders, under an identity header of its own, with a composer bound to that patient. The practice attention queue remains the companion's other mode.

## Decision record

Date: 2026-09-21.

Status: accepted.

UI-7d moved the practice prescribing queue into the right companion and stopped there.
The owner's reaction on seeing it was the correct one: *"Why have a medication order tab
without it... I need to be able to pick a patient in the side canvas."*

That is not a preference, it is a defect report. The practice queue is
**structurally empty in every checkout** — a transmission is refused before a
`prescription_transactions` row is written when no e-prescribing adapter is enabled —
so a companion whose only content was that queue offered a clinician nothing to do with
a prescription, ever. [D-092](./D-092.md) recorded the queue's own surfaces as
unexercisable and considered the matter named. It did not notice that naming the gap
still left a tool on the rail that does nothing.

### Decision 1 — the companion reaches the other half of prescribing, by patient

Prescribing has two halves and only one of them needs a vendor.

- The **cross-patient attention queue** answers "which of my prescriptions never reached
  a pharmacy?" It cannot be populated without an enabled integration.
- A **patient's own prescribing work** — intents staged, ready to authorize, ready to
  send, awaiting an external outcome, needing operational review — is populated by
  ordinary clinical work. Staging and authorizing need no adapter at all; only
  transmission is refused. It was already implemented, already correct, and reachable
  only by navigating into the chart's Medications section.

The selector at the top of the companion switches between them. Picking a patient renders
the same `PatientPrescriptionWork` the chart renders — one component, two callers, the
rule [D-089](./D-089.md) set — so the two surfaces cannot disagree about what a clinician
may do to a prescription. The panel stays a container and carries no copy of either
queue's rules.

### Decision 2 — picking a patient sets what the pane is about; it never tags a form

The objection to a patient picker on a prescribing surface is real and is the reason
there is still no dropdown *inside* the composer: choosing who a half-composed
prescription is for is how prescriptions land on the wrong chart.

This is the opposite arrangement. The selector chooses **which patient's record the pane
is showing**, before anything is composed, and every request that follows carries that
patient's id explicitly — `x-ehr-patient-id` on the read, an explicit `patientId` into
`orders.openComposer`, and a server that re-derives the patient from the record itself in
`patient-action-binding.ts` and refuses a mismatch. The composer opens already bound and
titled with the patient; there is nothing on it to pick.

The companion deliberately does **not** reuse `onOpenOrderCart`, which resolves the
*active* chart. A companion that can be pointed at someone other than the chart in front
of the clinician must not open a composer that resolves the chart instead.

### Decision 3 — a pane showing another patient says so

`AGENTS.md` requires an identity header per independently usable patient pane rather than
one label for the application, and this is exactly the case it exists for: the companion
can be on Sofia Martinez while Maya Chen's chart fills the canvas.

So the pane carries name, MRN and date of birth of its own, and when its patient differs
from the active chart it states that in words — *"This is Sofia Martinez, not the chart
in front of you (Maya Chen). Every action below is submitted against Sofia Martinez's
record."* The risk is not that a clinician may choose a patient; it is that they may not
notice which one is chosen. Naming it is the mitigation, not forbidding the choice.

### What this does not change about D-092

[D-092](./D-092.md)'s unexercised list stands exactly as written. The **practice queue's**
detail pane, patient-context gate, retry and evidence forms still need a non-empty
`prescription_transactions` and are still unreachable here. Nothing in this record
simulates a transport.

What changes is that the companion is no longer *only* that. The per-patient half is
driven end to end by a browser test on a deleted database — pick a patient, open the
composer bound to them, stage a real prescription through `/api/orders`, and watch it
arrive in the companion's own list as "Ready to authorize" — so the tool on the rail now
has provable content even while the queue beside it is empty.

### Consequences

- `WORKSPACE_ORDER_CREATED_EVENT` was declared with the rest of the workspace events and
  never dispatched by anything. Staging a prescription now dispatches it, because the
  companion is the first surface where a clinician can stage an order and watch the same
  panel go on saying the patient has no prescribing history. Any surface showing a
  patient's orders can now stay current.
- `PatientPrescriptionWork` was written for the chart's column and sets its own heading
  and body sizes for that width. Docked, the companion gives it about a third of that, so
  its type is scaled there — scaled rather than hidden, because the text it would be
  tempting to drop is the one explaining that prescription intent and transport stay
  separate from the medication record, which is the distinction the surface rests on.
- The composer still has no patient control of its own, and should not acquire one.
