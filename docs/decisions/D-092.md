## Decision record

Date: 2026-09-21.

Status: accepted.

[D-090](./D-090.md) decided that Prescribing's owner is the right companion and then
declined to implement it, because the queue is structurally empty in every checkout and
a replacement that cannot be exercised is not a proven replacement. That reasoning is
preserved and is not withdrawn. What changed is a decision the owner made, and one fact
that was checked rather than assumed.

### Decision 1 — the move proceeds on the owner's instruction

The owner's instruction, 2026-09-21: *"We don't need to buy DrFirst to get moved what is
there. Please move it and then delete it from its current position."* This follows the
standing instruction recorded in ROADMAP that no money is going to paid integrations, so
D-090's second entry condition does not resolve by waiting — it resolves by deciding
whether the move is worth making without it.

It was raised as a concern and answered. It is the owner's call, and the roadmap has
always said so: the ordered plan is taken in order *unless the owner explicitly overrides
scope*.

### Decision 2 — the bar D-090 set was the wrong bar, and the right one is lower

This is not a rationalisation of the override; it is what the override made worth
examining, and it holds independently.

D-090 applied the migration invariant — a destination is removed once its replacement has
**verified parity** — as though parity meant *the capability demonstrably works*. Parity
is a comparison, and the thing being compared is the route that is being removed. The
Clinical menu's Prescribing opened `PrescriptionOperationsWorkspace` against the same
empty `prescription_transactions` table. Its detail pane, its patient-context gate, its
retry and its evidence forms were exactly as unreachable from the menu as they are from
the companion. **Nothing that worked before stops working**, which is what the invariant
protects.

The real risk D-090 was circling is a different one and deserves to be stated properly:
once the companion is the only route, a fault in the half nobody can see fails silently.
Three things answer it, and none of them needed a vendor:

- **One component, two callers.** The companion renders the same
  `PrescriptionOperationsWorkspace` the module renders, at two densities, exactly as
  [D-089](./D-089.md) required of Tasks. The unreachable half is not a second copy that
  could differ from a reachable one; it is the same code on the same fetch. A unit test
  asserts the companion carries no copy of the queue's rules.
- **The tab is still there.** Retiring a menu entry is not retiring a surface. The
  `prescribing` module stays registered and the companion escalates to it, so a saved
  layout holding it is not stranded and a clinician who wants the full canvas still has
  it.
- **The per-patient half already has a working owner.** The chart's Medications section
  renders `PatientPrescriptionWork` with the same authorize, send, retry, confirm, renew
  and record-recovery-evidence actions for that patient. If the cross-patient queue were
  broken, no prescribing capability would be lost — only the cross-patient view of it.

### Decision 3 — the defect D-090 recorded is repaired by the move, in one code path

D-090 recorded that "Activate patient chart" calls `openPatient`, which clears the active
module and unmounts the workspace the clinician was working from, while every other
control on the detail pane is disabled because `patientContextMatches` is always false
from that surface.

The repair is the move, as D-090 said it should be, and it is written as one path rather
than two: before activating the chart, the queue asks for the Prescribing companion to be
put in front of the clinician (`WORKSPACE_OPEN_COMPANION_EVENT`, a generalisation of the
one-off `WORKSPACE_OPEN_COMMUNICATIONS_EVENT` that UI-5 left behind). From the companion
that request is a no-op; from the module it moves the queue somewhere that survives what
happens next. Either way the clinician ends with the chart active and the queue beside it.

**This line is not exercised by any test in this repository**, because reaching it needs a
selected item in a queue that cannot be made non-empty. A source-level test asserts the
ordering — the companion is asked for *before* the chart is activated — which is the part
that would be wrong if someone reordered it later. That is less than a browser proof and
is recorded as less.

### What is proven, and what is not

Proven in a browser, on a deleted database:

- The companion opens docked from the rail and renders the authoritative queue's own
  summary counts, its integration notice and its empty state — the queue itself, not a
  summary beside it.
- Expanded, it holds the main canvas with the queue and evidence panes, and the companion
  rail stays reachable (RIGHT-05).
- Escape redocks before dismissing.
- **The queue stays on screen while a patient chart is the active tab**, and the shell
  resolves that tab to the patient. That is the precondition every recovery action reads,
  and the one a full-canvas module can never produce. It is the whole argument for the
  companion, and it is now demonstrated rather than reasoned.
- The workspace tab still opens from the companion and renders the same component.
- Prescribing is offered nowhere in the top navigation, and the work navigation now has no
  expandable group at all.

**Not proven, and unprovable here:** the detail pane, the patient-context gate's own
controls, retry, and the three evidence forms. Reaching any of them needs at least one row
in `prescription_transactions`, and a transmission is refused before a row is written when
no prescribing integration is enabled. Nothing in this slice simulates a transport to
produce one; [D-090](./D-090.md)'s rejection of that stands unchanged and is the part of
it this record does **not** amend.

There is an honest route to exercising them that needs no vendor, and it is recorded in
ROADMAP rather than taken here: an enabled configuration for the development placeholder
adapter makes the adapter's own refusal a real `failed` transaction, which is a genuine
queue item with nothing fabricated about it. It is not taken because two things must come
first — the product has no surface at all for enabling an integration, and the operations
queue would then report "Integration ready" for an adapter that cannot transmit, which is
a worse honesty failure than the one being solved.

### Consequences

- UI-7 is complete. Clinical is gone, and every one of its five children has an owner:
  Patients, Documents and Labs in the `+` launcher; Tasks and Prescribing in the right
  companion.
- The Level 1 work navigation now holds three direct destinations and no menus, which is
  most of what UI-8 was going to do. UI-8 becomes the smaller cleanup it was always meant
  to be.
- A saved rail from before the companion existed is backfilled with Prescribing. Unlike
  the Communication and HR backfills, which were discoverability, this one prevents an
  outright loss of capability: the Clinical menu was the last route for those layouts.
- The unexercised surfaces are named here and in ROADMAP. A later slice that enables the
  placeholder adapter honestly should exercise them and say so; until then, no one should
  read UI-7d as evidence that prescribing recovery works end to end.
