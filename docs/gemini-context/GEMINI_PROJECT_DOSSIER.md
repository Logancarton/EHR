## 1. Mission and philosophy

Most EHRs are collections of database forms. When AI is added, it usually arrives as a chatbot in a side drawer, disconnected from the work.

Clinical Bond starts from the clinician's workflow:

- **Browser-like workspace.** Each patient chart is a persistent, labeled tab, as in Chrome. A clinician can keep several charts open and switch without losing their place.
- **Zen to Cockpit.** The same workspace scales from a distraction-free writing surface (a psychotherapist's note) to a dense multi-metric view (a high-volume psychopharmacology day). No single density is imposed.
- **AI as a layer, not a destination.** The goal is an operator that understands intent ("open Maya Chen's meds", "what was her last lithium level?") and acts inside the workspace. Structured clinical data stays authoritative; AI output is derived assistance.

Core model: `Clinician intent (voice/text/direct) <-> Dynamic workspace (Zen to Cockpit) <-> Structured clinical state <-> Ambient AI substrate`.

The canonical requirements are in [`../PRODUCT_VISION.md`](../PRODUCT_VISION.md), and the project rules in [`../../AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md).

## 2. Non-negotiable rules

1. **Patient = workspace, not page.** Charts stay open as tabs; each independently usable pane carries its own patient identity header.
2. **Progressive disclosure, never hidden essentials.** Minimalism removes repetition. It must not shrink essential text, hide patient identity, or replace labels with icon memorization.
3. **Never invent patient facts.** Nothing clinical is pre-filled, assumed or fabricated: no history, findings, diagnoses, medications, labs or scores. Two defects of this kind have been found and fixed. Rating scales used to open pre-answered ([D-102](../decisions/D-102.md)). New notes used to open with a normal mental status exam that said "no suicidal ideation" ([D-104](../decisions/D-104.md)).
4. **Explicit clinician action for anything legal, clinical or external.** Signing a note, sending an order, transmitting a prescription or submitting a claim always requires an authorized person to act. Staging is never sending.
5. **Provenance.** Documents carry a SHA-256 hash and version history. Signed notes are frozen snapshots with a record hash. Corrections append; they never rewrite.
6. **Vendors behind adapters.** E-prescribing, labs, clearinghouses and payments sit behind interfaces in `app/adapters/`. The core model never mirrors a vendor's schema.
7. **Patient-scoped companions.** Right-rail tools (Clinical AI, calculators, scratchpad, tasks, labs) bind to the chart in front. When a non-patient workspace (Billing, Intake, Home) is in front, chart-bound actions are *parked* with a visible banner rather than silently retargeted ([D-098](../decisions/D-098.md)).
8. **Calm two-level shell.** A global top bar (Home, omnibox, account) sits above the labeled workspace tabs and their `+` launcher. There is no left navigation rail.

## 3. What is built, and what is not

### Stack

- Next.js 16.3 (App Router, Turbopack), React 19, TypeScript 5.9 strict.
- Vanilla CSS with design tokens; no Tailwind or UI framework.
- SQLite (Node's built-in driver) with an explicit, append-only migration registry in `app/server/db/migrations/`.
- Tests: Node's test runner — 487 tests in 133 files, run by `npm run check` — and Playwright, with about 200 browser tests in 42 spec files.

### Built and working on synthetic data

- Multi-chart workspace, `+` launcher, persistent tabs, per-pane identity headers, layout customizer with density modes.
- Today dashboard: schedule roster with status filters (including **Late**), arrivals, outstanding work (unsigned notes, lab orders, refills, handoffs), team collaboration.
- Encounter note: document-style editor, autosave with revision protection, template phrases, explicit Review & Sign, immutable signed snapshots, append-only amendments.
- **Visit readiness** ([D-100](../decisions/D-100.md), [D-104](../decisions/D-104.md)): a panel beside the note that projects documentation gaps, safety, billing setup, coverage, lab monitoring and open care loops from the records that own them. Nothing is checked off by hand. A current safety-scale flag leads the list until the Risk Assessment is written.
- **Medication monitoring policy** ([D-099](../decisions/D-099.md)): layered intervals (system starter → practice default → provider override → patient exception with a documented reason). It drives attention prompts only; it never orders tests.
- **Billing preparation** ([D-101](../decisions/D-101.md)): charge templates per note template, a practice fee schedule, attested add-on codes sealed into the signed note, and an audited superbill.
- Vitals and lab results stored and entered separately ([D-103](../decisions/D-103.md)); author-owned scratch notes.
- Documents with versioning and SHA-256 provenance; rating scales (PHQ-9, GAD-7, ASRS v1.1, C-SSRS) that start blank and cannot be saved until complete.

### Not built, or not connected

- **No hosted AI model is connected.** The omnibox and Clinical AI answer from a local planner plus grounded reads of authorized records, with provenance shown. Free-form generation, ambient scribing from real audio and model-based extraction are designed for but not live.
- **No live vendor transport.** E-prescribing (DrFirst is the selected vendor; DoseSpot and Surescripts stubs exist), EPCS, Quest/Labcorp, clearinghouse claims, eligibility and payments are adapters without contracted connections. The UI says so wherever it matters. For example, Billing states that claims cannot be submitted.
- No patient portal, and no production infrastructure (HIPAA hosting, backups, secrets management). **No real PHI may be used.**
- Safety lives in the note's Risk Assessment prose. A structured risk record is not built.

## 6. Open questions for a reviewer

1. **Structured risk assessment.** Safety is now grounded in the written Risk Assessment. What minimum structured record (ideation, intent, plan, means, protective factors, disposition) would serve psychiatric practice without turning the note into a form?
2. **Readiness without distraction.** How should the readiness panel behave while the clinician is talking with the patient: what stays visible, and what waits until review?
3. **Coding support versus upcoding pressure.** How should documentation-to-MDM support be phrased so it helps accurate coding without nudging toward a higher level?
4. **Monitoring breadth.** What should the monitoring policy cover next (for example clozapine ANC, lithium with ACE inhibitors or NSAIDs, stimulant vitals), and how should interactions be expressed?
5. **Density.** Where do the Zen and Cockpit densities still fall short in these screenshots?
6. **Where AI should act first.** Once a model is connected, which workflow would most reduce clerical load while staying reviewable?
